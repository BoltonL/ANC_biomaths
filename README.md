# Socioeconomic Determinants of Antenatal Care Utilization in Sub-Saharan Africa  
**Contributors:** Morgan du Plessis, Georgie Roussos, Larisse Bolton  
*Year of study:* 2026  

Antenatal care (ANC) is a vital component of maternal and child health, significantly reducing risks during pregnancy and childbirth. The World Health Organization recommends a minimum of four ANC visits (recently updated to eight) to ensure better health outcomes for both mother and child.
Despite global efforts, ANC coverage remains suboptimal in many low- and middle-income countries, particularly in Sub-Saharan Africa. Socioeconomic and geographic disparities persist, and understanding the key predictors of ANC utilisation can inform targeted policy interventions.  

**Research Question:**   What are the socioeconomic and demographic factors associated with antenatal care utilisation (e.g., at least four visits) among women in Sub-Saharan Africa?  
**Objectives:**  
1.	To explore antenatal care utilisation patterns across selected Sub-Saharan African countries.
2.	To identify associations between ANC attendance and maternal age, education, wealth index, and urban/rural residence.
4.	To visualise disparities in ANC coverage across regions or countries.
6.	To draw policy-relevant conclusions based on the analysis.

This repository provides version control for all project-related documents, scripts and data.
